# Three phone concepts, round 2: no inherited rules

Written 30 Sep 2026. Thomas: "Rewrite it, drop all rules, go full creative"; round 1's three directions
were "not special enough". So none of these starts from the Mobile Amendment, the stacked-section skeleton,
a transaction list or a card. Each takes one material and makes the whole home screen out of it:
**language**, **time**, **matter**. All three share `sample-data.json` (MOCK, SAMPLE FIGURES) so they can be
compared on the same money.

What still holds, because it is product truth and not style: no figure the data did not supply, every
figure shown in full, no emoji, and the app never moves money.

## A. Sentence (the material is language)

The home screen is one sentence, set large, and nothing else:

> You have **£31.77 a day** until **the loan on 12 January**.

A second, quieter sentence carries the tension: *At this fortnight's pace it runs out on 29 December.*

- Every bold phrase is a door. Tap one and the sentence **rewrites itself in place** into its own
  derivation: "£7,315.76 you can reach, minus £4,011.43 already promised to rent, Spotify, Three and
  the gym, over 104 days". Each term in that is a door too (tap "rent" and the four rent dates unfold
  inline). Tap the background to fold back. The explain sheet from round 1 becomes the whole interface.
- Swipe up for the next sentence (what you are worth, how much is foreign, what changed since you
  last looked). There are about six sentences in total. That is the whole app at a glance.
- Press and hold anywhere to ask. "Can I afford a £45 ticket on Friday?" The sentence rewrites itself
  as the answer: "Yes. It moves 29 December to 28 December."
- Solid underline for what happened, dotted for what has not happened yet.

Why it is new for a phone: finance apps put the number on screen and a caption under it. Here the
caption is the interface, and the derivation replaces the chart.

## B. Horizon (the material is time)

The home screen is a landscape seen side-on. Left to right is time, from 60 days ago to the next
loan payment; height is free money.

- Today is a standing line a third of the way in; drag the land (or tilt the phone) to walk forward.
- Each bill is a **cliff** with its name at the edge. The loan is a wall rising at the far right. The
  **sea** is zero: where the land meets the sea is the zero day.
- The past is solid ground. The future is dotted (it has not happened yet).
- Pinch, or turn the pace dial, to set how much you spend a day; the future terrain reshapes
  live and the shoreline moves. At £36.40 a day the land drowns on 29 December, 14 days
  before the wall. At £31.77 it reaches the wall.
- The cross-section shows strata: each account is a layer, labelled in its own currency.

Why it is new: forecasts are line charts you read. This is a place you walk through, and the one
question that matters (do I reach the next income) is a coastline you can see.

## C. Sediment (the material is matter)

The phone is a glass vessel, and your reachable money is sediment in it: **one grain = £10**, about
730 grains.

- Layers settle by account, coloured from the theme, with the native currency at each layer's edge.
- Money already promised (rent, subscriptions to the next income) sits at the bottom as **locked
  grains**, drawn dotted. Only the free grains above them move.
- Tilt the phone and the free grains slide; the promised ones do not. You can feel how much is actually loose.
- "Run the days": drag down and the vessel drains day by day at your recent pace, a trickle out of
  the bottom, with the date counting. At this pace it runs dry on 29 December, before the loan.
- Tap a day's spend and that many grains fall out.

Why it is new: area already encodes value in Numeris; here area becomes a physical quantity
you can tilt and watch drain.
